# Hooks, openings, closings

## The 210 rule

LinkedIn shows roughly **210 characters** before "see more". That preview is the entire pitch. Most readers decide there.

A blank line ends the preview early. That is a lever, not a bug:

- **Play A (short hook):** one strong line, then a blank line. Preview is short and creates a gap the reader has to close. Best for confessions, blunt claims, questions.
- **Play B (dense hook):** three lines with no blank line between them, ~200 chars total. Preview carries real payload, so the reader is already invested before the click. Best for war stories and teardowns.

The roll's `OPENING` implies which play fits. Report the actual character count before the cut in the NOTES block.

## The 8 opening types

**cold-number** — Lead with the figure, no setup.
> `Two days. One missing await.`

**mid-scene** — Drop the reader inside the moment. No context first.
> `The logs said the tool call succeeded. The database said nothing had happened.`

**flat-confession** — Admit the thing plainly, no cushioning.
> `I did not know what a race condition looked like until Thursday.`

**counterintuitive** — A claim that contradicts what the reader expects.
> `Adding more context made my agent worse.`

**genuine-question** — Ask something you actually do not know the answer to.
> `How do you decide when an agent should stop retrying?`

**blunt-sentence** — One short declarative. No hedging.
> `Most agent bugs are not model bugs.`

**real-quote** — Something a person actually said to you.
> `"It works on my machine," I said, and then it did not work on my machine.`

**small-image** — The literary lane. A concrete image that turns out to be the technical point.
> `My tea went cold three times before I found it.`

---

## Never open with

- "I'm excited to share" / "Thrilled to announce" / "Happy to share"
- "Recently I was thinking about..."
- "As a developer," / "As someone who..."
- "Have you ever wondered..."
- Any greeting. Any hashtag. Any emoji.
- A sentence that describes the post instead of starting it.

---

## Closings

Matched to the roll:

- **open-question** — Ask about *the reader's* experience, not about your post. "What is the longest you have chased a bug that turned out to be one character?" beats "What do you think?" A question about their own story is the single strongest comment driver, because people would rather talk about themselves.
- **flat-stop** — End on the last fact. No summary, no lesson. Feels abrupt and that is correct.
- **unresolved-loop** — Leave something open. "I still do not know why the first attempt worked."
- **small-confession** — End smaller than you started. "Anyway. Two days."
- **one-line-takeaway** — One specific line, tied to this story only. Never a universal maxim.

**Never close with:** "Agree?" / "Thoughts? 👇" / "Let me know in the comments!" / "Follow for more" / a motivational sentence that would fit any post.

---

## Links

A URL in the post body costs roughly **60% of reach**. Never put one in the body.

Put it in the first comment. If the post needs to point at something, end with a plain line: `link is in comment`. Then surface the URL in the NOTES block so it can be pasted as the first comment.

---

## Hashtags

Zero to three, at the very end, on their own line. Specific and narrow beats broad.

Good: `#llm #agents #debugging`
Bad: `#innovation #technology #motivation #coding #developer #softwareengineering`

Zero is often the right number.

---

## Comment strategy (worth telling the user, once)

- The first 60 minutes decide reach. Comments in that window are weighted 4 to 10x.
- A reply of 15+ words counts about 2.5x more than a short one, so answer commenters with real sentences, not "Thanks!".
- When one comment thread gets 4 or 5 replies, the post gets pushed to people who do not follow you.
- A save is worth roughly 5 likes. Posts with a reusable specific in them (a command, a number, a checklist) get saved.
