# Humanize

The core file. Two moves: **subtract** the AI tells, **add** human texture. Both are required. Doing only one produces text that is either sterile or sloppy.

---

## Part A — Never (AI-tell blacklist)

Any of these in a draft means rewrite that line.

**Punctuation**
- Em dash. Ever. Use a comma, a period, or `...`
- Semicolons in a casual post.
- A colon introducing a list of exactly three things.

**Sentence shapes**
- `It's not X, it's Y.` And every cousin: "This isn't about X. It's about Y."
- `Not only... but also...`
- Three-item lists of adjectives or benefits ("faster, cleaner, and more reliable").
- A rhetorical question followed immediately by its own answer, twice in one post.
- Perfectly balanced sentence pairs. Two clauses of the same length mirroring each other.
- Starting consecutive sentences with the same word for effect.

**Phrases**
- "Here's the thing." / "Here's what I learned." / "Here's the kicker."
- "In today's fast-paced world" / "In the world of X"
- "Let that sink in." / "Read that again."
- "The result?" / "The takeaway?" / "My advice?"
- "game-changer, leverage, delve, robust, seamless, unlock, elevate, harness, landscape, realm, testament, crucial, vital, pivotal"
- "At the end of the day"
- "I'm excited to share" / "Thrilled to announce"

**Structure**
- Every paragraph the same length.
- A closing universal lesson that could be pasted onto any post ("Progress over perfection.").
- Emoji bullets. `🔹 ✅ 🚀 💡`
- A stack of 5+ hashtags.
- Bolding one phrase per paragraph.
- An intro sentence that announces what the post will contain.

## Part B — Human texture menu

The roll picks two. Apply exactly those two. Not more, not fewer, and not the same two next time.

1. **Fragments.** Like this. Two days. For one missing `await`.
2. **Start with And / But / So.**
3. **Self-interrupt.** "I fixed it. Well, fixed is a strong word."
4. **Inconsistent numbers.** "three retries" in one line, "2 seconds" in the next.
5. **`...` mid-thought.** Real hesitation, not drama.
6. **Keep one filler.** actually, basically, honestly, I mean.
7. **One much longer sentence** sitting among short ones, running past where it should have stopped because that is how thinking actually sounds when you have not edited it.
8. **A useless concrete detail.** The tea went cold. It was 2am. It was raining.
9. **A real name.** `gpt-4o-mini`, `Zod`, `ECONNRESET`, `pnpm`, the actual error string.
10. **An aside in parentheses.** (still not sure why)
11. **Repeat a word.** "It was slow. Slow slow."
12. **Trail off.** Leave a thought unfinished where the reader can complete it.
13. **Admit incomplete understanding.** "I still don't fully know why that works."
14. **Small self-contradiction, left standing.** "It's a small thing. Cost me a day, so maybe not."

## Part C — ESL markers (Level 2: exactly one per post)

These are what make it read like a real Bangla speaker writing good English, not like a machine writing perfect English. Use the one the roll selected.

- **Dropped article.** "I opened terminal and just sat there." / "Worst part is nobody noticed."
- **Present perfect for simple past.** "I have read that line maybe 20 times." / "Yesterday I have tried three different fixes."
- **Slightly-off collocation.** "stuck in this problem" (not *on*), "searching the reason", "I was in doubt", "give a try"
- **Direct-translation phrasing.** "After that I have understood one thing." / "That day I learned it in hard way."
- **Redundant subject or doubled that.** "This bug, it took me two days." / "I think that that is the real reason."

**Hard rules:**
- Never in the first line. The hook must land clean.
- Never inside a technical claim. Accuracy is credibility.
- Never two in one post at Level 2.
- Comprehension must be 100% unaffected. A reader should feel the accent, not stumble.
- Vary the *position* across posts: sometimes line 3, sometimes the second-to-last line, never always the same slot.

At `rough 1`: skip Part C entirely.
At `rough 3`: use two, from different categories, far apart in the post.

---

## Part D — Punctuation profile

- No em dash. Not once.
- Prefer a period where a comma would do. Short sentences read faster and hold dwell time better on mobile.
- Blank line between most paragraphs, **but not all**. One place where two lines sit together with no gap is a strong human signal, because a real person stops formatting when they get into the thought.
- One-line paragraphs are fine. Several in a row is fine. All of them is a pattern.
- Lowercase after a colon is fine. Missing Oxford comma is fine.

---

## Part E — Why the variation matters

A single humanized post is easy. The failure mode is the fifth post, when a reader who has seen all five starts to feel the recipe: same length, same rhythm, same confession in paragraph four, same question at the end.

So the roll is binding. If it says `micro-essay` with a `flat-stop` closing and the instinct says a war story with a question would perform better, follow the roll anyway. Variance across the set is worth more than the local optimum of any one post.
